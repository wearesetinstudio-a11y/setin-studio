  In the 19th century, Polish botanist and explorer Józef Warszewicz braved the uncharted wilderness of Central and South America. Operating well beyond the technological limits of his time, he documented thousands of plant species previously unknown to European science, securing a lasting place in botanical history. His relentless field work helped catalog rare orchid species that are now celebrated as part of Panama's natural identity.

Rather than relying on dry historical textbook tropes, we translated his journey into a sharp, contemporary digital format. Combining archival research with generative visual art, we recreated the atmosphere of dense tropical jungles to capture viewer attention instantly and bridge two distant heritage narratives.

    title: "Low attention spans and visual archive scarcity"
    text: |-
      Capturing attention in fast-paced social feeds presents a massive hurdle for historical content. In an environment built around instant entertainment, the opening second had to halt scrolling immediately to prevent the story from being bypassed.
      
      The lack of visual archival material posed another major challenge. Historical records of Warszewicz's expeditions consist mostly of black-and-white botanical sketches and handwritten field notes. We lacked high-resolution, vibrant footage that could faithfully capture the raw beauty of the Panamanian wilderness.
      
      Finally, cultural nuance was essential. The script needed to feel natural, balanced, and respectful to audiences in both Eastern Europe and Latin America, avoiding one-sided perspectives or overly solemn commentary.

    title: "Generative world-building and universal storytelling"
    text: |-

      To bridge the archival gap, we engineered a custom generative art direction pipeline. We generated photorealistic sequences depicting 19th-century field expeditions and untouched tropical ecosystems, ensuring a cohesive cinematic atmosphere grounded in historical detail.
      
      We anchored the core narrative in universal human themes: curiosity, perseverance, and passion for the natural world. This allowed the story of a Polish explorer to resonate naturally across geographical and cultural boundaries.

Superando los límites técnicos de su época, documentó miles de especies vegetales desconocidas en Europa, convirtiéndose en un referente de la botánica científica. Su trabajo de campo permitió describir orquídeas autóctonas que hoy forman parte del patrimonio natural y el orgullo nacional de Panamá.
      
      Con el propósito de revivir su legado y visibilizar este vínculo entre ambas naciones, desarrollamos una pieza audiovisual en formato corto. Nos alejamos del formato documental tradicional para adaptar la narrativa a los códigos visuales contemporáneos. Mediante dirección de arte apoyada en herramientas generativas, integramos datos históricos con secuencias cinematográficas de la selva tropical, creando una pieza de alto impacto que conecta culturas a través del diseño.
